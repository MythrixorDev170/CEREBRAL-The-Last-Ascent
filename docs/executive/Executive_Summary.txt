Executive Summary
We propose a comprehensive final pass to turn the existing CEREBRAL: The Last Ascent prototype into a 
fully polished reference build. This entails preserving all working gameplay while enhancing visuals,
audio, UI, and horror elements. Key fixes include refining spacecraft inertia, improving weapon
accuracy and recoil, syncing dialogue captions with speech, and enforcing the narrative rules that 
Cerebral’s voice is internal and enemy comms appear only after scanning. We will integrate the rich 3D
reference assets for the player ship, enemy classes, and the Cerebral planet, establish formal Asset
and Color Bibles, and expand world content (stations, wreckage, missions) with modular kits. Enhanced
horror content (ghost ships, blood-stained environments, subtle AI manipulations) will psychologically
challenge the player. Free CC0 resources (Poly Haven textures/models【14†L84-L90】, Kenney assets,
Freesound effects) will be used. 
The following report details all gaps and fixes, proposes math and code patterns for smooth physics
and feedback, and outlines a development roadmap. We provide a final single-shot Claude prompt
and a separate Jabali-from-scratch brief. We include tables of priorities, asset/color bibles, a QA
checklist, and mermaid diagrams for system architecture, timeline, and story flow. This becomes the
definitive design document guiding both the reference build and the later Jabali implementation.
Analysis & Gap List
We evaluated the current prototype and reference materials. The foundation is solid (WASD flight, chase
camera, shields, multi-phase enemies, scan/hack, missions, mobile support). However, critical gaps
remain in feel, visuals, audio, and narrative. Below are the prioritized issues (P0 highest) and the exact
fixes required:
P0 – Core Gameplay & Feel (Must Fix)
Flight Inertia / Cruise Speed: Issue: The ship loses speed too quickly when not thrusting,
making non-boosted cruise feel sluggish and comparable to a Drone’s speed. Fix: Reduce passive
drag. Use delta-safe smoothing: for example,
const dragFactor = 0.995;
P.vel.multiplyScalar(Math.pow(dragFactor, dt));
This preserves forward momentum (unlike the current aggressive .55 factor). Player velocity should
gradually bleed off, not drop abruptly【14†L84-L90】. This lets the player maintain combat speed
without constant boosting.
Weapon Accuracy & Recoil: Issue: Spread/recoil often cause missed shots that feel like “wasted
bullets.” Fix: Reduce base spread at range; apply limited controlled recoil as a visual kick, not
large aim deflection. Use predictive aiming for locked targets:

Dialogue Sync: Issue: Captions often appear before or after the speech. Fix: Use a unified
dialogue function. E.g., 
let utter = new SpeechSynthesisUtterance(line.text);
utter.onstart = () => showSubtitle(line.text);
utter.onend = () => hideSubtitle();
speechSynthesis.speak(utter);
This ensures subtitles and voice start/stop together. Queue lines to avoid overlap and handle
interruptions gracefully.
Narrative Rules:
Cerebral’s Voice: Must always be an internal voice, not coming from any in-world speaker. Label
dialogue as “Cerebral” and apply audio filtering to suggest “in-head” origin【7†L161-L169】.
Enemy Communication: Ships do not broadcast lore text by default. Only after scanning an enemy
do we unlock its comms (distress calls, chatter) to avoid info dumping. Before scan, an enemy
shows only generic markers and attacks.
HUD Customization: Add a HUD Settings menu. Players can toggle elements (health, radar,
mission log, subtitles) and adjust size/opacity. Provide presets (Full, Combat-Focused, Minimal)
and allow keyboard remapping (forward, fire, scan, etc.) as currently supported, ensuring logical
action bindings. Changes must persist.
Tutorial & Help: Implement an interactive tutorial mode accessible from the menu. In tutorial,
spawn simple targets or dummy drones to let players practice thrusting, dodging, firing, locking,
scanning, and hacking. This fixes the current text-only tutorial by giving players hands-on
experience.
Save/Load: Extend beyond 2 slots. Offer 3–5 save slots with timestamps and descriptions (e.g. 
Safe Point: #2). Verify that saving/loading preserves progression, inventory, upgrades, and visited
events.

Objective Progression: First mission must lead to Cerebral properly. After scanning the initial
derelict, a hackable terminal reveals “Unknown Planetary Signal.” On hack success, add a new
map waypoint. Cerebral’s planet should only appear as a distant objective at mission end, not

P1 – Visuals and Assets (High Priority)
Asset Integration: Replace all placeholder geometry with the high-fidelity models from our
reference images: 
Player Ship: Build the hero ship with the silhouette, cockpit, wings, engine pods, etc. indicated in
our concept sheet. Use MeshStandardMaterial with metallic=1.0, roughness ~0.3 on hull; black
glass on cockpit. Add emissive stripes and engine glow (cyan). Ensure a damage-pass texture
(scratches/burn marks) for hull damage.
Enemies: Create distinct models for Drone, Soldier, Elite, Warden as per the reference sheet. For
example, Drones are small winged interceptors; Soldiers are medium fighters with amber
insignia; Elites have purple highlights; Wardens are large crimson-command ships. Each with a
visible multi-stage shield generator component (glowing hub).
Ghost Ship: Design a derelict cruiser (darkened, twisted lights, bloodied metal). It should be
noticeably different (age, style, hull damage) from active enemies.
Cerebral Planet: Drastically expand the existing “network planet” into a massive artificial
structure. Add ring structures, orbital arrays, huge antennas, glowing energy conduits across its
surface. Use large-scale textures and emissive cores. The planet’s colors should be harsh (black
rock with glowing cyan/red).
LOD/Performance: Use 1–2 LOD levels: full detail on close-up (≈200–300 triangles per ship),
reduced poly at distance. Aim for ~60–100K triangles total across scene. Optimize using
instancing/geometry merging for repetitive debris.

Lighting Presets: Implement scene presets (Directional sunlight + ambient rim light) for each

point/spot lights (cold white for lab, warm amber for military), toggled by mission state
(emergency red flickering).
Shadows: Use selective shadows. Key objects (player, near walls) cast shadows; avoid shadow￾casting for every point light to save performance【14†L84-L90】. Use ambient occlusion (baked
or SSAO) for depth.
Space Environment:
Enrich the backdrop. Keep starfield and nebula as background, but add midground: scattered
asteroids with detailed textures (rock + ice), ship wrecks (half-buried asteroids, fallen satellites),
distant spacecraft silhouettes (capital ships seen far). 
Use volumetric fog sparingly for planets (e.g. a faint gas ring around Cerebral) to add depth.
Foreground: floating debris and smaller asteroids that drift when ships pass, emphasizing scale.
Asset Kit: Build modular pieces (wall segments, pipes, consoles) for stations so we can create
varied rooms (labs, docking bays) quickly. A mix of metal panels, consoles, cables, vents should
form the research labs and military halls. 
UI / HUD Polish: Maintain clarity with hierarchy. Group HUD elements:
Center-bottom: Reticle/target info.
Bottom-left: Player status (shield, hull bars, energy).
Top-right: Radar/minimap (circular or cone view).
Top-left: Mission/objective.
On-target: Floating info (target name, distance, lock indicator). Use subtle animations on updates
(e.g. blink shield bar when hit). Ensure the HUD is legible at all camera distances. On mobile,
scale and spacing must be adjusted (we have that partly done).

Sound Design: Layered, spatial audio is critical. Use CC0 or self-created samples:
Player engine hum + thrust surge (obtain from Freesound or synth; license check). Engine pitch/
volume increase with throttle. Boost adds a distinct whoosh.

Weapon sounds: Laser “pew” (short beam sound), missile “whistle” on travel, explosion booms
(different sizes). Impact sounds: shield “zing” (sci-fi zap), hull “clang/drip” (metal impact).
Environmental: Station ambient hum, pipe/radio interference, station alarms (siren). In space:
distant rumble, electromagnetic static.
Spatialization: Use positional audio (Three.js AudioListener / PositionalAudio ). Sound
volume/fade based on distance: e.g., 
let dist = listener.position.distanceTo(source.position);
let vol = 1 / (1 + Math.pow(dist/20, 2));
This mimics inverse-square law【9†L170-L178】 but clamped to keep subtle distant ambiance.
Horror Audio: Cerebral’s voice with reverb, slight distortion. Occasional whispers or static.
Heartbeat thump when heavily damaged. Silence and high-frequency stingers in key moments
to jolt players.
Horror Content: Build on the existing terror system:
Blood/Stains: In lab/refuge areas, place sporadic blood trails, handprints on walls, overturned
chairs, and inert corpses. These cues suggest prior violence. (Keep it implied; no gore spatter on
screen.)
Ghost Encounters: Expand the derelict “ghost ship” event. For example: a deserted research
ship drifting silently. As the player approaches, its lights flicker on. A disembodied log plays (“…I
hear whispers… did something move?”) and then static. If the player scans, data appears
corrupted. The ship vanishes or reappears elsewhere. This should be rare and optional but
memorable.
AI Manipulations: During missions, introduce subtle anomalies: flickering instrument panels,
doors that close behind spontaneously, mission audio with a beat or echo. At extreme tension,
the background may distort (e.g., screen vignette pulse on damage).
Cerebral Dialogues: Write lines that comment on player choices (dodge patterns, weapon use)
and philosophical remarks (“Why do you fight? Are you any different?”). Use sparse distribution:
let silence speak between threats【7†L191-L199】. Always present lines AFTER events (e.g. “You
destroyed them quickly.” after killing enemies).
Jumpscare Use: Very limited; e.g. dark corridor with a sudden distant figure. More often rely on
suspense: what’s unseen is scarier【7†L197-L200】.

Each mission should have clear objectives and justification (e.g. “We need that scientist’s data.”).

Code Architecture & Quality
The current prototype is one large HTML/JS file. For future development and especially for Jabali
recreation, document modular responsibilities:
InputHandler, FlightController, CameraController, CombatSystem, ProjectileManager, 
EnemyAI, ShieldSystem, HackingSystem, MissionManager, HorrorManager, UIManager, 
SettingsManager, SaveManager.
Eliminate global variables where possible. Use classes or modules (if migrating to ES6 or similar
in Jabali).
Performance: reuse objects (pools for projectiles, particles). Limit per-frame allocations. Profile
for memory leaks (especially due to Web Audio nodes or Three.js objects).
Maintain code comments (especially before each system function) and consistent formatting for
readability.
